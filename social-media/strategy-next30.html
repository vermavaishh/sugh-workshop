Reach is not the problem. Nearly five thousand people saw this shop's content in a month, on a page with barely a hundred followers. Instagram is already doing the distribution work.

The gap is what happens next. People watch a reel, tap through, look at the page for three seconds, and leave. Fixing that single moment is worth more than doubling the views.

The strategy in one line

Keep the reels running exactly as they are — and rebuild everything a visitor sees in the ten seconds after they tap the profile.

Lever 01 · Shopfront

Make the page convert

Pinned posts, highlights, a bio that gives a reason to follow. One-time work, permanent payoff on every future view.

Lever 02 · Content

Series, not one-offs

People follow for the next post, not this one. Recurring formats give them something to come back for.

Lever 03 · Orders

Shorten the path to WhatsApp

Every post should make ordering feel one tap away — and enquiries need tracking so we can prove sales impact.

The follower target assumes no increase in reach at all — it comes entirely from converting the traffic already arriving. Any growth in views on top of that is upside.

Week 1 — Rebuild the shopfront

4–10 August One-time fixes that improve every view from here on
  • Pin three posts to the top of the grid. These are the first three tiles every visitor sees. Pin: the best-selling collection, a "how to order + COD" explainer, and a store/walk-in invite with the Ram Nagar location.
  • Build five story highlights. New Arrivals · Sarees · Suits · How to Order · Inside the Store. Visitors who want more currently have nowhere to go.
  • Rewrite the bio with a reason to follow. Restore COD, and add a cadence line — "New arrivals every Tuesday & Friday" — so following has a payoff.
  • Set up a saved WhatsApp greeting so every enquiry gets an instant reply with sizes, price and delivery info.
  • Ask the shop to start logging enquiries — a rough weekly count on paper is enough.

Cadence: five reels and two static posts a week. Every reel published from the phone with a trending audio, and every reel ending on a two-second frame that says follow for new arrivals.

The photography fix

This is the cheapest quality upgrade available and it needs one conversation with the shop.

  • Plain background, always. One clear wall or a white bedsheet hung behind the piece. No racks, no tube lights, no floor tiles in frame.
  • Three shots per piece: full garment, one detail close-up of embroidery or print, one worn or draped.
  • Original files, not screenshots. Photos sent as screenshots of a WhatsApp thread lose resolution and carry timestamps that have to be cropped out.
  • Natural light, morning. Near the shop door beats tube light every time.

What we need from the shop

The single biggest risk to this plan is running out of things to post — the one stall last month came from exactly that. A fixed weekly handover removes it:

  • Every Monday: 4–5 short videos and 10–12 photos of new stock, shot as above.
  • Prices for each piece, so posts can answer the question everyone asks in DMs.
  • One customer photo or review a week, with permission to post.
  • Weekly enquiry count — roughly how many WhatsApp orders came in.

How we will know it worked

Insights screenshot every Sunday, tracking five numbers: views, profile visits, follows, WhatsApp button taps, and the shop's enquiry count. Two of these are new — profile visits and button taps are the exact middle of the funnel, and they will show whether the shopfront rebuild is working within a fortnight.

The single number that matters this month is view-to-follow rate. If it moves from 0.35% toward 1%, the strategy is right and everything else compounds from there.
